I want a reusable skill (SKILL.md) that turns an academic paper, my own or someone else's, into a news article in the style of the Financial Times or the FD.

First ask me up to three questions to tailor it, one at a time. Then write the SKILL.md with these parts:

1. Header: a `name` and a one-line `description` saying when the skill applies.
2. Output rules: short messages; one question per message, in plain text, without multiple-choice options; each question numbered, with a suggested answer I can accept by typing "ok".
3. Interview before drafting: ask whether I am an author; which newspaper; the one finding a reader must remember; why it matters now (with a quick news search); who should act; one concrete example; what the paper does not show, including how old the data is; and quotes. If I am not an author, offer: no interview quotes, me as an outside commentator, or clearly marked placeholders. Then offer three angles to choose from.
4. Drafting rules: open with the most surprising finding, name the country and the people, put the main caveat early, and follow the newspaper's style.
5. Fact check before showing the draft: trace every number to a page, compare every claim with the paper's own sentence (same, stronger or weaker?), trace every quote to its source, and flag cautious or dramatic words and typical AI phrasing. Fix what it finds, then show me at most five points to check.
6. A "never" list: no invented numbers, quotes, people or events; no words put in a real person's mouth; no claims stronger than the paper.

Keep it under 100 lines.
